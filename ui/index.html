menu_book ConnectPhone Guide

A simple guide to connecting, controlling, and troubleshooting your Android phone from your Mac.

rocket_launch Start here

ConnectPhone has one main job: create a reliable ADB connection between your Mac and Android phone. After the connection is ready, you can mirror the screen, send input, and view device health information.

  1. Install Android platform tools and make sure the phone is charged.
  2. On the phone, open Settings → About phone and tap Build number seven times.
  3. Open Developer options and enable USB debugging. Enable Wireless debugging only when you want Wi‑Fi control.
  4. Open ConnectPhone and connect by USB first, or use wireless pairing in the Connection Center.
  5. Accept the “Allow USB debugging” prompt on the phone. Select “Always allow” only on your own Mac.

usb USB connection

USB is the easiest first connection and is usually the most stable option.

  1. Connect the phone to the Mac with a data-capable USB cable.
  2. Unlock the phone and approve the debugging prompt.
  3. Open Connection Center. The phone should appear under connected devices.

If it is not found: try another cable or USB port, keep the phone unlocked, and revoke old debugging authorizations in Developer options before reconnecting.

wifi Wireless connection

Wireless debugging requires the Mac and phone to be on the same Wi‑Fi network. Pairing is normally needed only once per network.

  1. On the phone, open Developer options → Wireless debugging.
  2. Choose Pair device with pairing code.
  3. Enter the displayed IP address, pairing port, and six-digit code in ConnectPhone.
  4. For later connections, use the phone’s wireless debugging IP and ADB port in the Connection Center.
  5. Use Connect & Keep Reconnecting to save the endpoint and enable trusted reconnects.

Security: ConnectPhone verifies the enrolled device identity before automatic reconnect. A changed or unknown device is rejected.

desktop_windows Mirror & Stream

Open Mirror & Stream after the phone is connected. Choose a mode and start it. The phone screen opens in a separate mirror window.

  • Mirror screen: control the phone from the Mac.
  • Camera stream: use the phone camera as a live source.
  • Audio only: listen without displaying video.
  • Record session: save the mirrored session to the Mac.

For smoother wireless video, use a strong 5 GHz network and lower resolution or bitrate if the stream stutters.

monitoring Metrics & preferences

Live Metrics shows battery, uptime, system load, and connection latency. Use the ping test when wireless control feels slow.

Preferences controls video quality, frame rate, audio routing, buffering, orientation, screen behavior, and saved PIN settings.

PINs are stored in the macOS Keychain. Leave a PIN field empty when you do not want to change the saved value, then click Save Preferences.

build Troubleshooting checklist

  • Phone missing: unlock it, reconnect USB, check USB debugging, and accept the authorization prompt.
  • Wireless connection fails: confirm both devices use the same network and that Wireless debugging is enabled.
  • Connection drops: keep the phone awake during setup, use a stronger Wi‑Fi signal, and reconnect once manually to enroll the current device identity.
  • Mirror does not start: disconnect duplicate ADB entries, reconnect one device, and try a lower video setting.
  • Controls do not work: enable the Android security/debugging permission required by your phone manufacturer.
  • Still stuck: click Restart App, then check the Connection Center status and the Live Metrics ping result.

wifi Connection Setup Guide

ConnectPhone supports both high-speed USB and seamless Wireless connections. For the best experience, we recommend pairing wirelessly over a 5GHz network.

Prerequisites

1. Go to your phone's Settings > About Phone and tap "Build Number" 7 times to unlock Developer Options.

2. Navigate to Settings > System > Developer Options.

3. Enable USB Debugging and Wireless Debugging.

4. Important for Mac control: You must also enable "USB debugging (Security settings)" to allow macOS to simulate screen taps and keystrokes.

Wireless Pairing (First Time)

1. Ensure your Mac and phone are on the exact same Wi-Fi network.

2. Tap directly on the text "Wireless Debugging" on your phone to open its menu.

3. Tap "Pair device with pairing code". A pop-up will show an IP address, Port, and a 6-digit Wi-Fi pairing code.

4. In ConnectPhone's Connection Center, type these exact details into the Pairing section and click Pair.

Connecting (Subsequent Uses)

Once paired, you never need to pair again. Simply ensure Wireless Debugging is turned on, go to the Connection Center, and click Scan Network to magically find and connect your phone!

desktop_windows Screen Mirroring & Audio

Control your phone with zero latency right from your Mac using our built-in mirroring engine powered by scrcpy.

Controls & Hotkeys

• Right Click: Acts as the "Back" button.

• Middle Click: Acts as the "Home" button.

• Scroll Wheel: Scrolls lists and pages natively.

• Cmd + O: Turns your phone's physical screen off while keeping the mirror active (saves battery!).

Audio Routing

By default, Android 11+ routes device audio directly into your Mac. You can disable this or change the target audio output device (like AirPods) from the Preferences tab before starting a mirror.

sync Universal Clipboard & Media

Pulling Screenshots

In the Media & Sync tab, click Fetch Latest. ConnectPhone will scan your phone's standard screenshot directories (DCIM/Screenshots and Pictures/Screenshots). Clicking "Pull" will instantly download the image directly to your Mac's Desktop!

Background Clipboard Sync

Clicking "Start Background Sync" creates a headless, invisible bridge between your devices.

Phone to Mac: Instantly copy text on your Android and press Cmd + V on your Mac.

Mac to Phone Workaround: Since Android 10, Google severely restricts background apps from altering the clipboard for security reasons. To get text from your Mac to your phone without opening a mirror window, we provided the "Type Mac Clipboard on Phone" button. Simply tap a text field on your phone and click the button to inject your Mac's clipboard as secure keystrokes.

fingerprint macOS Touch ID Integration

The Biometric Daemon is an exclusive ConnectPhone feature that bridges Apple's Secure Enclave with Android's lockscreen.

How it Works

If you have this preference enabled, the dashboard continuously monitors your phone's screen state. The moment you wake up your phone to the lockscreen, or encounter an App Lock (like WhatsApp or Banking apps requiring a PIN), your Mac will instantly prompt you for Touch ID. Upon successful authentication, it decrypts and securely injects your Android PIN to unlock it instantly.

Setup & Troubleshooting

Make sure you have saved your correct Android PIN in the Preferences tab. If the daemon stops responding, simply turn your phone screen off and on again to re-trigger the wake event.

folder_shared Storage Manager & Keyboard Controls

ConnectPhone features a desktop-native Storage Browser allowing you to browse, search, preview, and download files directly from your Android device.

Key Storage Features

• List/Grid View Toggle: Click the view layout icon next to Refresh to instantly swap between a detailed List view and a clean Grid view.

• Real-Time Search: Use the search input bar to instantly filter files and folders in the current directory.

• Multi-Select & Batch Actions: Check multiple files or folders using the selection checkboxes. A floating batch options bar will slide up from the bottom, enabling batch deletion or batch downloading (compiled directly as a ZIP archive).

• Native macOS Downloads: To bypass browser sandboxing limitations inside the app window, all downloads are saved directly to your Mac's ~/Downloads directory, and the app automatically triggers macOS Finder to highlight and reveal the saved file or ZIP archive.

• Bidirectional Drag & Drop:
1. Mac to Phone: Drag multiple files or folders from macOS Finder and drop them anywhere into the Storage Browser panel to upload them sequentially.
2. Phone to Mac: Drag any file row directly out of the application window and drop it onto your Mac Desktop or Finder folder to download it instantly.

Image Preview Gallery Modal

Double-click any image file (JPG, JPEG, PNG, WEBP, GIF, BMP) to open it inside a compact, glassmorphic centered overlay card. The preview modal supports zooming in/out, 90-degree rotations, next/previous slides, and direct native download.

Full Keyboard Navigation Map

Maximize your workflow with full keyboard navigation controls (automatically disabled when typing inside search or path input fields):

• Arrow Up / Arrow Down: Navigates and highlights focused items in the active directory with automatic smooth scrolling.

• Enter: Enters the selected directory, or opens the image previewer if an image is highlighted.

• Spacebar: Checks or unchecks the selection checkbox of the highlighted item.

• Backspace / Delete: Navigates back up to the parent directory (acts as the Back button).

• Escape (in Preview): Closes the image preview gallery modal.

• Arrow Left / Arrow Right (in Preview): Navigates to the previous or next image in the directory.

Trackpad Gestures Map

• Two-Finger Swipe Left-to-Right: Navigates back up to the parent directory (acts as the Back button) in the file browser.

• Two-Finger Swipe Horizontal (in Preview): Swipes horizontally to browse the previous/next image inside the previewer.

• Pinch-to-Zoom (in Preview): Standard trackpad pinch-in and pinch-out gestures dynamically zoom the active preview image.
